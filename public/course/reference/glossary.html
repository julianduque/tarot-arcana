Glossary

Definitions follow this course’s declared usage. Where a word belongs to several traditions, the distinction matters more than memorizing a short synonym.

Arcana
Plural of arcanum, a mystery or secret. In this course, Major Arcana names the 22 trumps; Minor Arcana names the 40 pips and 16 courts. The terms do not establish an ancient origin for the cards.
Attribution / correspondence
A relationship assigned within a named symbolic system: a trump to a letter, a pip to a decan, a suit to an element. An attribution can guide interpretation without proving a real-world claim.
Book T / Liber LXXVIII
Book T names Golden Dawn tarot material. Liber LXXVIII, published in The Equinox I:8 in 1912, transmits that material with editorial interventions. A printed recension or later transcription is not necessarily identical to every manuscript.
Court card
One of sixteen rank-and-suit figures. A court can describe a person, a role, an approach, or a relation; age, gender and appearance are not compulsory decoding rules.
Decan
One of thirty-six ten-degree zodiac sectors. In the Golden Dawn tarot scheme, each belongs to a pip from Two to Ten and has a planetary ruler. A sign contains three decans. Aces have no individual decan.
Deviant Moon
Patrick Valenza’s deck. Use its images and creator booklet directly; the course’s Hermetic overlays are study tools, not proof that Valenza encoded each attribution. VIII is Justice; XI is Strength.
Dignity, astrological
A planet’s condition according to an astrological scheme, such as domicile, exaltation, detriment or fall. It is not interchangeable with elemental dignity, nor with the planetary ruler of a tarot decan.
Dignity, elemental
The relative support or weakening assigned to elements in a declared reading method. This course uses a simplified modern matrix, comparing each neighbour with the centre. Strength is not the same as beneficence.
Element
Fire, Water, Air or Earth as a symbolic quality, not a chemical substance. Suit correspondences in this course are Wands–Fire, Cups–Water, Swords–Air, Pentacles/Disks–Earth.
Emanation
A metaphysical account of differentiation from a source. Used here to discuss Hermetic readings of the sephiroth; it is not a physical mechanism or a claim demonstrated by the diagram.
Golden Dawn
A late nineteenth-century occult order whose teachings synthesized tarot, Hermetic Qabalah, astrology and other materials. Its correspondence scheme is this course’s organizing framework, not the original historical structure of fifteenth-century tarot.
Hermetic Qabalah
A Western esoteric adaptation and synthesis drawing on Jewish Kabbalah, Christian Cabala and other traditions. The spelling signals usage here; it does not erase their shared history or differences.
Interpretation
A reasoned account that relates imagery and a stated symbolic framework to a question. Distinguish it from observation, a quoted source, and independent evidence about the world.
Lightning Flash
A diagrammatic sequence through the ten sephiroth from Kether to Malkuth. Its visual route is not itself one of the 22 trump paths, and is not identical to the Fool’s Journey.
Major Arcana / trump
One of the deck’s 22 trumps. In the Golden Dawn framework these occupy 22 connecting paths and carry letter and astrological attributions. Their printed numbers are not path numbers.
Mothers, Doubles and Simples
A division of the twenty-two Hebrew letters into three, seven and twelve in Sefer Yetzirah. Recensions and translations differ; the Golden Dawn’s exact tarot/planet mapping is a later application.
Number card / pip
In this course, Ace through Ten of each suit. Number/sephirah, suit/element, image and, for Twos through Tens, the decan all contribute. Number times element is a starting prompt, not an exhaustive meaning generator.
Path
Here, one of 22 connecting lines between sephiroth, numbered 11–32 in the Golden Dawn correspondence table. In other contexts, “32 paths” includes the ten sephiroth. Line crossings without circles are not extra sephiroth.
Planetary order
The decan sequence cycles Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon; Aries begins at Mars within that cycle. Weekday order is different and is generated through an additional planetary-hour scheme.
Querent
The person whose question is being explored. In a self-reading, reader and querent are the same person. Their agency and right to disagree remain part of the reading.
RWS
Rider–Waite–Smith: the deck associated with publisher Rider, A. E. Waite, and artist Pamela Colman Smith, issued in 1909. All embedded card images in this course are RWS images.
Reversal
A card that appears upside down relative to the reader. Its treatment depends on the method chosen before the reading. An upright card can already express difficulty; a reversal is not automatically its opposite.
Sefer Yetzirah
The Book of Formation, a Jewish cosmological text with several recensions and a substantial commentary tradition. It does not present a tarot deck or the course’s complete Golden Dawn correspondence chart.
Sephirah / sephiroth
Singular/plural for the ten numbered spheres of the Tree. The course uses Hermetic interpretations such as Kether, Crown, and Malkuth, Kingdom. Aces through Tens are placed by number within each suit’s World.
Significator
A card deliberately chosen to represent the querent or subject, in methods that use one. This course’s modern Celtic Cross does not require an extra significator; Waite’s published method does.
Spread
A set of positions and questions established before drawing. Position, card, neighbouring cards and the querent’s context interact. A spread’s labels are part of the method, not an inherent property of the cards.
The Fool’s Journey
A modern pedagogical and interpretive narrative connecting the trumps. The course’s three acts use the RWS sequence. They are useful for comparison without being an ancient universal initiation syllabus.
Thelema
The religious and philosophical system associated with Crowley, central to Thoth’s revisions and imagery. A Thelemic explanation belongs to that tradition; adopting it is not required to compare the decks.
Thoth
The deck developed by Aleister Crowley and Frieda Harris. Label its names and numbers explicitly: Adjustment VIII, Lust XI, Art XIV, Aeon XX, Universe XXI. Thoth Knight corresponds to RWS King in this course’s elemental crosswalk; Thoth Prince corresponds to RWS Knight.
Tree of Life
A diagram of ten sephiroth and their relationships. The displayed interactive Tree uses Golden Dawn paths and endpoints. Switching the comparison to Thoth does not transform the diagram into a Thoth path map.
Worlds, Four
Atziluth (Emanation/Fire/Wands), Briah (Creation/Water/Cups), Yetzirah (Formation/Air/Swords), Assiah (Action/Earth/Pentacles). Distinguish this suit layer from the court rank’s own element and seat.
YHVH / court family
A Hermetic fourfold arrangement used for Fire/Father, Water/Mother, Air/Son and Earth/Daughter. These traditional relational titles describe a symbolic model; the reader need not impose a gender role on a real person.

Glossary

The course's shared vocabulary. Terms are used consistently across every lesson — when a word is defined here, it means exactly this. Grouped by area; skim it, don't memorize it.

The deck's architecture

Tarot deck. 78 cards: 22 Major Arcana + 56 Minor Arcana.

Arcanum (pl. arcana). Latin for "secret." A card, framed as a "mystery." The plural names the two halves of the deck.

Major Arcana. The 22 trump cards, numbered 0–21 (the Fool to the World). The archetypal, spiritual "big" forces. Also called trumps or keys.

Minor Arcana. The 56 remaining cards: four suits of 14 (ten numbered "pips" + four court cards). The texture of daily life.

Suit. One of four families of the Minor Arcana: Wands, Cups, Swords, Pentacles. Each maps to an element.

Pip. A numbered Minor card, Ace through Ten (the Ace = 1). Forty in total (10 × 4 suits).

Court cards. The 16 "people" cards — in Rider-Waite-Smith: Page, Knight, Queen, King of each suit. (Thoth renames them; see below.)

The four suits & elements

Wands. Element Fire. Will, drive, energy, spirit, creativity, ambition. (Thoth keeps "Wands"; playing cards: Clubs.)

Cups. Element Water. Emotion, love, relationship, intuition, the inner life. (Playing cards: Hearts.)

Swords. Element Air. Intellect, thought, language, conflict, truth, decision. (Playing cards: Spades.)

Pentacles. Element Earth. Body, money, work, material world, the tangible. Called Disks (or Coins) in the Thoth and Marseille traditions. (Playing cards: Diamonds.)

Deck names

Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS). The 1909 deck designed by A. E. Waite, illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith, published by Rider. The most influential modern deck; its fully-illustrated pips changed tarot. Public domain — the art in this course.

Thoth. Aleister Crowley's deck, painted by Lady Frieda Harris (1938–43, published 1969). Built explicitly on the Golden Dawn system; renames some cards and courts. Still under copyright.

Deviant Moon. Patrick Valenza's modern (2008) deck; surreal, psychological imagery. Follows RWS structure. Under copyright.

The esoteric spine

Correspondence. A mapped relationship between a card and something in another symbolic system — an element, a planet, a zodiac sign, a Hebrew letter, a place on the Tree of Life. The engine of Hermetic tarot.

Hermetic Qabalah. The Western esoteric adaptation of Jewish Kabbalah used by the Golden Dawn; the framework onto which tarot is mapped. (Spelled Qabalah here to mark the Hermetic version, vs. religious Kabbalah.)

Tree of Life. The central Qabalistic diagram: 10 sephiroth (spheres) joined by 22 paths. The 40 pips map to the sephiroth; the 22 Majors map to the paths.

Sephirah (pl. sephiroth). One of the ten spheres on the Tree, numbered 1–10 (Kether to Malkuth). Each Minor number corresponds to one.

Path. One of the 22 lines connecting sephiroth, each assigned a Hebrew letter and a Major Arcanum.

Tetragrammaton. The four-letter name of God, יהוה (Yod-Heh-Vav-Heh). Its four letters map to the four elements/suits and to the four court ranks — the "fourfold key."

Four Worlds. Atziluth, Briah, Yetzirah, Assiah — four Qabalistic levels of reality, mapped to the four suits/elements (Fire, Water, Air, Earth).

Decan (decanate). A 10° slice of the zodiac; 36 in all (12 signs × 3). The 36 pips numbered 2–10 each rule one decan, with a planetary sub-ruler.

Book T. The Golden Dawn's tarot document — the source of the "standard" astrological and Qabalistic attributions used across RWS and Thoth.

Elemental dignity. A reading technique: neighbouring cards strengthen or weaken each other by whether their elements are friendly, hostile, or neutral.

Reading terms

Querent. The person a reading is for (may be the reader themselves).

Spread. A defined layout where each position has a fixed meaning (e.g. past/present/future).

Significator. A card chosen in advance to represent the querent or the question.

Reversal. A card drawn upside-down; read as blocked, internalized, or shadow-side meaning (optional — many readers don't use them).

Fool's Journey. A narrative reading of the 22 Majors in order as the soul's progress from innocence (0) to integration (21).

Reading list and source guide

Read a relevant passage with a card in front of you. A source earns its place by clarifying a question, not by increasing the size of your bibliography.

A workable reading order

  1. Start with the free Waite entries and your RWS deck. Compare image, Part II symbolism and Part III divinatory lists.
  2. Add one interpretive companion, such as Pollack, and use a notebook or Greer’s workbook for repeated practice.
  3. Read Book T / Liber LXXVIII as lessons 5–9 introduce the structure. Use Wang or Fortune for extended Hermetic explanations.
  4. For Thoth, pair Crowley with DuQuette and the actual cards. For Deviant Moon, pair your observations with Valenza’s booklet or companion.

Use books you already own before adding more. Reading assignments name sections or cards because pagination varies by edition.

Primary texts and historical orientation

Primary texts tell us what an author wrote. They do not settle every historical claim that author made, reveal every artistic decision, or validate a prediction. Online transcriptions can contain errors; check printed diagrams and disputed wording against your edition.

Companions for sustained study

Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom

Rachel Pollack · Modern interpretive commentary

Read alongside the Major and suit studies for a psychological and mythological approach. Compare her interpretation with your own observation and Waite’s lists; use it as another developed reading, not a substitute for the deck.

Tarot for Your Self; The Complete Book of Tarot Reversals

The wider shelf

The earlier course also listed Paul Foster Case’s The Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages, Regardie’s The Golden Dawn, Aryeh Kaplan’s Sefer Yetzirah, Katz and Goodwin’s Secrets of the Waite-Smith Tarot, Sallie Nichols’s Jung and Tarot, Meditations on the Tarot, and Robert M. Place’s The Tarot: History, Symbolism, and Divination. Keep them as optional avenues after identifying a specific research question. These are different kinds of works: order documents, Jewish commentary, psychological interpretation, Christian meditation, and historical synthesis should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

How sources are used here

  • Card observations and worked examples are course analysis. Examples described as selected or constructed are teaching arrangements, not reported predictions.
  • Golden Dawn, RWS, Thoth and modern practice labels keep systems distinct. Correspondence facts do not by themselves establish a card’s whole meaning.
  • Assignments to modern books guide your further reading; they do not claim that every edition or chapter was reproduced or checked in full.
  • Thoth comparisons draw on primary text and named commentary. Use your own deck to inspect Harris’s paintings; an RWS image cannot illustrate a claimed Thoth detail.
  • Deviant Moon prompts stay anchored to your physical deck and creator material. A symbolic overlay is identified as your experiment.

Original card scans and attribution records remain in the authoring workspace. The course displays Pamela Colman Smith’s RWS artwork through Tarot Arcana’s shared card assets.

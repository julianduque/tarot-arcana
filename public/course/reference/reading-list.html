The Reading List

The books behind this course, annotated and ordered by how you'd actually use them. Every lesson's "Learning resources" box points here. Badges: Free readable online now · Your deck speaks to a deck you own · Approachable Demanding Formidable difficulty.

How to use this. You don't need all of it. Start with the two under Start here; add from The esoteric spine as the Correspondences lessons send you; reach for Reflective & psychological once you're reading for real people. One deck in your hands the whole way.

Start here

Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom

Rachel Pollack  ·  Approachable

The deepest card-by-card reading of the Rider-Waite-Smith ever written, and the reason most serious readers of this deck are serious. Holds Jungian archetype, Qabalah, myth, and the plain visual evidence in view at once without collapsing into any of them. The single best companion to the whole Major/Minor arc of this course.

How to read itOne card a day with the physical card in front of you. Write your own reading of the image first, then read hers — the gap is your curriculum.

The Pictorial Key to the Tarot

A. E. Waite  ·  Free Approachable

The deck's own author on his cards — short, arch, and deliberately reticent. Waite was a sworn Golden Dawn initiate describing images built to encode material he'd promised not to disclose, so read it as much for its silences as its statements: the omissions are where the Correspondences track later goes. This course's default primary source.

How to read itKeep a running list of everything Smith drew that Waite declines to explain. That list is your map into the esoteric spine.

The esoteric spine

The Qabalistic Tarot

Robert Wang  ·  Demanding

The clearest systematic mapping of the cards onto the Tree of Life in print, and unusually honest: Wang sets the Golden Dawn, Case, and Crowley attributions side by side rather than pretending one is self-evident. The single most on-mission book for the Correspondences track; the reference you'll keep for years.

How to read itAlongside the Tree-of-Life and Four-Worlds lessons. Where sources disagree, record both — those are the tradition's live questions, not errors.

buy · Amazon →

The Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages

Paul Foster Case  ·  Demanding

The most rigorous teacher the tradition produced, working from a deck (B.O.T.A.) consciously modelled on Smith's. Locks Hebrew letters, numbers, colour scales, and images together until the Majors stop being pictures and become one continuous argument. Caveat: Case teaches his own B.O.T.A. lineage — his colour attributions especially are specific to it — so read him with Wang, not as the last word.

How to read itSlowly, with pencil. Draw the Tree weekly until the 32 paths are automatic. Don't skip the numerical sections — they carry his real reasoning.

buy · Amazon →

The Golden Dawn  Book T inside

Israel Regardie (ed.)  ·  Formidable

The actual concealed source. Waite and Case were both working from the Golden Dawn's inner teaching; Regardie published it — including Book T, the order's tarot paper that this course's decan titles and attributions ultimately come from. If you ever want to read the primary document rather than an interpreter, this is it.

How to read itNot cover to cover. Go straight to Book T and the tarot sections; treat the rest as reference. Confirms (or complicates) every table on the correspondence sheet.

buy · Amazon →

The Mystical Qabalah

Dion Fortune  ·  Demanding

The classic readable introduction to the Tree of Life as a living system — the sephiroth and paths as states you move through, not a diagram to memorize. Read it to understand the Tree behind the tarot; it makes the Correspondences track click.

How to read itBefore or alongside the Tree-of-Life lesson. One sephirah at a time; sit with each before moving down.

buy · Amazon →

Sefer Yetzirah (The Book of Formation)

Anonymous, trans. Westcott & others  ·  Free Demanding

The ancient text that sorts the 22 Hebrew letters into 3 mothers / 7 doubles / 12 simples — which is the tarot's element/planet/sign scheme for the trumps (Lesson 8). Very short. Read the letter-classification passages; the cosmology around them is optional.

How to read itJust the sections on the three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples. Cross-reference to the Major Arcana on the correspondence sheet.

For the decks you own

Understanding Aleister Crowley's Thoth Tarot

Lon Milo DuQuette  ·  Your Thoth deck Approachable

You own the Thoth deck, and this is the sane, reliable way into it — DuQuette decodes Crowley's titles, colours, and the astrology printed on the cards without the density (or the ego) of Crowley's own Book of Thoth. The bridge between this RWS-taught course and your Thoth deck.

How to read itAfter the decan lesson (Lesson 7). Pull the matching Thoth cards and read the printed titles/glyphs with him as guide.

buy · Amazon →

Secrets of the Waite-Smith Tarot

Marcus Katz & Tali Goodwin  ·  Your RWS deck Approachable

Archival research, not speculation: the theatrical sources, period illustration, and Pamela Colman Smith's own circle that explain images Waite never accounts for. The corrective to a century of guessing — and it restores Smith as co-author, not illustrator-for-hire.

How to read itWith your list of Waite's evasions beside you; cross off every one this book answers. What survives is genuinely esoteric — that's where Wang and Case come in.

Reflective & psychological

Tarot for Your Self  (+ her wider work)

Mary K. Greer  ·  Approachable

The most important living author for your mission — reflective reading and reading for others, ethically. Tarot for Your Self is the workbook for using the cards on your own life; 21 Ways to Read a Tarot Card is the finest single method for going deep on one card; The Complete Book of Tarot Reversals is the reference behind Lesson 11. Greer is the practice-and-ethics half the "great system" books leave out.

How to read itDo the exercises — don't just read them. Start Tarot for Your Self alongside the Reading Practice track (Lessons 6, 12, 21).

buy · Amazon →her blog →

Jung and Tarot: An Archetypal Journey

Sallie Nichols  ·  Approachable

Depth psychology from someone with real analytic training (she worked with the C. G. Jung Institute), reading the Majors as a map of individuation. Her chapters on the Devil and the Tower are the finest psychological writing on those cards anywhere. Note: she works from the Marseille — read her argument, apply it to Smith's version, note what changes.

How to read itOne Major per week with your own dreams and reactions recorded beside her commentary. Pairs naturally with the Fool's-Journey lessons.

buy · Amazon →

Meditations on the Tarot

Anonymous (Valentin Tomberg)  ·  Formidable

Twenty-two letter-meditations, published anonymously and posthumously — Christian Hermeticism, alchemy, and mysticism worked through the Majors at a depth nothing else here approaches. Marseille-based, and it doesn't matter: the meditations are about the arcana, not any printing. A years-long book, and worth it. Not for information — for transformation.

How to read itOne letter a month, minimum. Don't take notes to master it. Reread until something shifts, then move on. Strictly optional — and unforgettable.

History & grounding (read one, early)

The Tarot: History, Symbolism, and Divination

Robert M. Place  ·  Approachable

The readable, honest one-volume grounding: where tarot actually came from (a 15th-century Italian card game), how the occult attributions were added later, and why the system is still meaningful once you know that. Inoculation against the pseudo-history ("ancient Egypt," etc.) that surrounds this subject. Read it early so the esoteric spine sits on real ground.

How to read itStraight through, near the start. It won't teach you to read cards — it teaches you what you're actually holding.

buy · Amazon →

On this list

This is an expansion of a good "seven essential Waite-Smith volumes" list — Pollack, Waite, Katz & Goodwin, Case, Wang, Nichols, and Tomberg are all genuinely first-rate and correctly placed. What I added, and why:

  • Mary K. Greer — the original list is heavy on studying the system and light on doing readings. Greer is the missing half, and closest to your mission (reflection, reading for others, ethics).
  • Regardie's Golden Dawn / Book T — if the thesis is "recover what Waite concealed," the concealed thing is the Golden Dawn material itself; here's the primary source, not just its interpreters.
  • DuQuette (Thoth) — you own the Thoth deck; a pure RWS list ignores it.
  • Robert Place (history) — grounding against pseudo-history, so the esoterica rests on fact.

One honest caveat about the framing it came with: the "Waite swore an oath and hid the key" narrative is true (he was an initiate, and the Pictorial Key really is evasive about the Qabalistic attributions) but it's dramatized in a "secret knowledge" register this course otherwise avoids. The facts hold; take the mystery-marketing tone with a grain of salt.

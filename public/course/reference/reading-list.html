Start here

A primary text and a card-by-card companion for the Major and Minor Arcana.

Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom

Rachel Pollack  ·  Approachable

Read alongside the Major and suit studies for a psychological and mythological approach. Compare her interpretation with your own observation and Waite’s lists; use it as another developed reading, not a substitute for the deck.

How to read itChoose the card you are studying in lessons 2–4 or 13–20. Write your own observations before reading Pollack, then record one agreement and one alternative.

The Pictorial Key to the Tarot

A. E. Waite  ·  Free Approachable

Waite’s text accompanies the Rider–Waite–Smith deck and is the course’s main primary source. Part II discusses the Major Arcana’s symbolism; Part III supplies divinatory meanings and a reading method. Compare his words with Smith’s images, and leave details he does not explain as open questions.

How to read itKeep the physical card beside the relevant entry. Compare Part II symbolism with Part III meanings; use the Celtic Cross passage alongside lesson 10.

The esoteric spine

Tree of Life, Hebrew letters, worlds and decans. These authors work in different traditions; retain those labels when comparing them.

The Qabalistic Tarot

Robert Wang  ·  Demanding

Use the Tree, Worlds and card sections to compare named systems. Keep the source column in every copied table. Its Hermetic synthesis is valuable for this course without representing every Jewish or occult tradition.

How to read itUse it beside lessons 5, 8 and 16. Copy a small part of a table, label its tradition, and trace the same relationship in the course’s correspondence explorer.

Publisher and edition details

The Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages

Paul Foster Case  ·  Demanding

A study of the Major Arcana through Hebrew letters, numbers, colour and symbolism in Case’s B.O.T.A. tradition. Use it as a named interpretive system. Compare its assignments with the Golden Dawn and Thoth columns rather than treating every table as interchangeable.

How to read itRead one Major at a time alongside lessons 8 and 13–15. Keep Case’s attributions in their own column when comparing systems.

The Golden Dawn

Israel Regardie (ed.)  ·  Formidable

A collection of Golden Dawn teaching documents, including the tarot material known as Book T. It gives context for the course’s decan titles and correspondences. The freely linked Liber LXXVIII is a separate historical publication of Book T material with editorial interventions; keep the edition and source visible in your notes.

How to read itBegin with the tarot sections, not the whole collection. In lessons 7 and 9, compare one pip title and one dignity statement with Liber LXXVIII.

The Mystical Qabalah

Dion Fortune  ·  Demanding

Read the Three Supernals, Four Worlds and selected sephirah chapters when lessons 5 and 16 become familiar. Read its metaphysics as Fortune’s Hermetic exposition. The named sections are more reliable across editions than invented page references.

How to read itTake one sephirah at a time alongside lessons 5 and 16. Write its function in your own words before applying it to a numbered card.

Publisher and edition details

Sefer Yetzirah: The Book of Creation

Aryeh Kaplan · Translation and commentary  ·  Demanding

Kaplan’s translation and commentary offers a Jewish study of the Sefer Yetzirah. The cover shown is Kaplan’s edition; the free Westcott translation linked below is a different text and editorial presentation. Neither should be treated as a tarot manual or collapsed into the course’s later Hermetic framework.

How to read itStart with the Mothers, Doubles and Simples for lesson 8. Label each note with the translation used, and distinguish the text from later tarot applications.

Lon Milo DuQuette  ·  Your Thoth deck Approachable

Read beside Crowley’s relevant card section and your physical Thoth deck. This offers a guide to its symbolism and attributions; distinguish DuQuette’s explanatory choices from Crowley’s own text.

Secrets of the Waite-Smith Tarot

Marcus Katz & Tali Goodwin  ·  Your RWS deck Approachable

A study of Pamela Colman Smith, A. E. Waite and the historical setting of their deck. Use its discussion of images and possible sources alongside close observation. Separate documented connections from proposed influences; an unexplained detail in Waite does not by itself establish a secret correspondence.

How to read itSelect one RWS card from the Major or suit studies. Compare a proposed source with what is actually visible in Smith’s composition.

Reflective & psychological

Workbooks and interpretive companions for practice, self-reflection and contemplation.

Tarot for Your Self

Mary K. Greer  ·  Approachable

Use the first as a workbook: write, revisit and test your readings. Use the second to compare reversal methods before selecting one. Keep the original draw and your first interpretation when revising a journal entry.

How to read itDo one workbook exercise alongside lessons 6, 12 or 21. For lesson 11, compare reversal methods in The Complete Book of Tarot Reversals and record the method you choose.

Publisher and edition details

Jung and Tarot: An Archetypal Journey

Sallie Nichols  ·  Approachable

A psychological reading of the Major Arcana through Jungian ideas and Marseille imagery. Read it as interpretive commentary, then compare the actual image in your RWS deck. The differences between decks matter; Nichols’s discussion is not evidence of Smith’s artistic intention.

How to read itTake one Major alongside lessons 2 or 13–15. Note your response first, then compare the Marseille and RWS images before applying the commentary.

Meditations on the Tarot

Anonymous (Valentin Tomberg)  ·  Formidable

Twenty-two meditations on the Major Arcana from a Christian Hermetic perspective, published anonymously and associated with Valentin Tomberg. This is an optional contemplative work, distinct from a divination handbook or a historical account of tarot’s origins.

How to read itRead one letter slowly when you want contemplative study. Keep its theological claims separate from your evidence for a practical reading.

Read a historical overview early, then use it to place later esoteric claims in context.

The Tarot: History, Symbolism, and Divination

Robert M. Place  ·  Approachable

A historical and symbolic survey that helps distinguish the early cards from later occult interpretations. Use it to orient the esoteric books on this list: an interpretation can be meaningful without being part of the cards’ earliest documented history.

How to read itRead the historical overview near the start of the course. Keep a short timeline separating surviving early cards from later esoteric developments.

How sources are used here

  • Card observations and worked examples are course analysis. Examples described as selected or constructed are teaching arrangements, not reported predictions.
  • Golden Dawn, RWS, Thoth and modern practice labels keep systems distinct. Correspondence facts do not by themselves establish a card’s whole meaning.
  • Assignments to modern books guide your further reading; they do not claim that every edition or chapter was reproduced or checked in full.
  • Thoth comparisons draw on primary text and named commentary. Use your own deck to inspect Harris’s paintings; an RWS image cannot illustrate a claimed Thoth detail.
  • Deviant Moon prompts stay anchored to your physical deck and creator material. A symbolic overlay is identified as your experiment.

Original card scans and attribution records remain in the authoring workspace. The course displays Pamela Colman Smith’s RWS artwork through Tarot Arcana’s shared card assets.

Use books you already own before adding more. Covers identify editions; a linked transcription may use a different edition. Reading assignments name sections or cards because pagination varies.

Tarot: The Hermetic Key

22 lessons · 78 card studies · Western esoteric traditions

Learn to observe a card, understand its symbolic relationships, and offer a coherent reading. Rider–Waite–Smith is the visual starting point; Golden Dawn correspondences supply the course’s Hermetic framework. Thoth comparisons and guided work with your Deviant Moon deck show where systems and images diverge.

How to study

Keep your RWS deck and a notebook nearby. In each lesson, read the core explanation, inspect the relevant cards, then do the exercises before checking the discussion. Work through a few card studies per sitting; a long suit chapter is a small sequence of sessions, not a speed-reading assignment.

Begin with the 22 lessons in order. Return to earlier cards as each new layer becomes available: image and question first, number and element next, then astrology and the Tree. You do not need to recall every correspondence before making a useful three-card reading.

Treat each reading as a reasoned interpretation. Separate what you see, what a named tradition says, and what you infer about the question. Your notebook should include alternative readings and revisions, not only interpretations that felt right.

Your study rhythm

Give each lesson a first reading and a separate practice session. The Major and suit chapters need several sittings. At the start of the next session, recall the previous distinction without notes; return to it about a week later. Interleave similar cards, such as Four of Swords and The Hermit, so recognition becomes more precise.

The capstone asks you to explain a complete reading, compare another plausible interpretation, and identify what the cards cannot establish. Finishing the pages does not establish mastery; your records show what you have practiced and what still needs work.

Keep the traditions visible

RWS means the deck by A. E. Waite and Pamela Colman Smith. Thoth means the deck developed by Aleister Crowley and Frieda Harris. Golden Dawn labels identify the particular Hermetic correspondence system used here. Deviant Moon exercises ask you to inspect Patrick Valenza’s actual imagery and consult his booklet where available.

Hermetic correspondences are historically developed interpretive relationships. They do not account for every symbol on every deck, or establish a single ancient origin for tarot.

The Trumps: Hebrew Letters & Golden Dawn Paths

Lesson 8 of 22 · Major Arcana · Reading ~5 min · Practice separately

Read the Hebrew-letter classification, distinguish it from later tarot assignments, and keep numeral, letter, astrology and diagram convention visible.

Twenty-two letters and a later tarot application

Sefer Yetzirah classifies the Hebrew letters into three mothers, seven doubles and twelve simples. The text concerns letters and cosmology; it is not an ancient tarot manual. Different recensions, translations and commentaries preserve different details. The linked Westcott translation is itself part of a Western esoteric reception history, so its planetary assignments should not be assumed to match the Golden Dawn table in every row.

The Golden Dawn application associates the three mothers Aleph, Mem and Shin with Air, Water and Fire and with Fool, Hanged Man and Judgement. The seven doubles take classical planets; the twelve simples take zodiac signs. This gives an organizing relation of 3 + 7 + 12. It does not establish that the fifteenth-century trumps originated as an illustration of this scheme.

Learn names and distinctions carefully

You will see transliteration variants: Beth/Bet, Gimel/Gimmel, Cheth/Chet, Teth/Tet, Tzaddi/Tsade and Tav/Tau. Choose a consistent course spelling but recognize alternatives. Hebrew is written right to left. A transliteration is an aid to recognition, not a substitute for learning the written letter if letter study is part of your aim.

Traditional letter-name meanings such as ox, house, door and water are often used by occult commentators. Keep those conventional symbolic glosses distinct from claims about the precise historical origin of every letter shape. A creative association between a letter and a tarot image should be identified as an interpretive association unless a primary text actually makes it.

The three classes provide a study order

Start with the mothers: Fool/Aleph/Air; Hanged Man/Mem/Water; Judgement/Shin/Fire in the Golden Dawn scheme. The absence of a fourth mother for Earth is a feature of this classification, not a missing trump. Extended systems give additional Earth or Spirit associations to some cards; those should be introduced with their source rather than squeezed into the three-letter count.

Then learn the seven planetary trumps: Magician/Mercury, Priestess/Moon, Empress/Venus, Wheel/Jupiter, Tower/Mars, Sun/Sun, World/Saturn. Finally study the twelve zodiac trumps. Keep the name of a card such as The Moon separate from its attribution: the Moon card is assigned Pisces, while the High Priestess is the lunar planetary trump. Confusing an image’s title with its astrological field creates avoidable mistakes.

A path has two named endpoints

Take the Empress in the Golden Dawn arrangement. Daleth and Venus are associated with the path connecting Chokmah and Binah. One interpretive approach considers generative relation between force and form. That does not mean the Hebrew word alone “proves” fertility or that every Empress in a reading refers to motherhood. The path supplies one relation among several that a reading may use.

Study an endpoint before inventing a story about the line. If you know only the names of the two spheres, begin with a modest account and consult their treatment in the source. Otherwise a path exercise can become twenty-two arbitrary narratives presented as necessary consequences of geometry.

Thoth: distinguish letter changes from printed numerals

Crowley assigns Emperor to Tzaddi and Aries, and Star to Heh and Aquarius. The comparison panel names these changes. The diagram remains the Golden Dawn map, in which Emperor/Heh is path 15 and Star/Tzaddi is path 28. Do not read its highlighted endpoints as an asserted Thoth diagram. Crowley’s prose and later diagrams require careful comparison before a path’s endpoint can be inferred merely from a letter exchange.

There is a separate Strength/Justice issue. Thoth VIII Adjustment and XI Lust retain Libra and Leo respectively, while RWS uses XI Justice and VIII Strength. The primary distinction is easy to see when numeral, title, letter and sign are separate fields. Keep them separate in your handwritten notes too.

A precise interpretive exercise

Choose Death. Record its printed numeral, the course’s Golden Dawn letter, astrological sign and path endpoints. Then describe the picture without those terms. Next write what the correspondences add: which relationship or question becomes more specific? Finally identify an interpretation that the card does not justify, such as predicting someone’s death. Studying an esoteric correspondence does not remove the need for restraint in application.

For now, reproduce the three classes and trace a few well-understood paths. Memorizing all paths is a longer project supported by the three Major Arcana studies. You can learn the architecture progressively without treating every lookup as a failure.

Retrieve before revealing

Does Sefer Yetzirah directly teach the Golden Dawn tarot table?

No. It supplies a letter/cosmological classification used in later applications. The text’s versions and translations also differ in details.

Read with the sources

Named historical translation: Sepher Yetzirah, translated by W. W. Westcott (1887). Read the chapters on Mothers, Doubles and Simples; distinguish the text, supplements and later tarot applications. This translation does not represent every recension.

Primary historical publication: The Equinox I:8 (1912), Liber LXXVIII: A Description of the Cards of the Tarot. Consult the card’s named section and attribution table. This publication transmits Book T material with editorial interventions; it is not an untouched original manuscript.

Primary text, archive transcription: Crowley, The Book of Thoth. Consult the named card entry in your edition; card studies provide additional section links. Compare the text with Harris’s painting, and distinguish later diagram conventions.

Comparative practitioner commentary: Robert Wang, The Qabalistic Tarot. Consult the Tree/Worlds discussion and the entries for your selected cards. Record the system and edition used for each table.

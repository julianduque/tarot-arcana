The Trumps' Hidden Alphabet: Letters & Paths

By the end of this lesson You'll know why the 22 trumps split into 3 + 7 + 12, read a trump through its Hebrew letter's picture and its astrological force, and understand the elegant structure that assigns elements, planets, and zodiac signs to the Major Arcana.

Why exactly 22?

You've seen that the 22 trumps are the 22 paths of the Tree of Life, each carrying one Hebrew letter. That's not a coincidence of counting — the Hebrew alphabet has exactly 22 letters, and an ancient Qabalistic text, the Sefer Yetzirah ("Book of Formation"), sorts them into three classes. That sorting is the tarot's element/planet/sign scheme.1

3 + 7 + 12 = 22. So a trump's "type" is set by its letter: an element-trump, a planet-trump, or a sign-trump.

Mem is the letter of water — and the Hanged Man hangs in suspension, reversal, the watery deep between one state and the next. Tav is the final letter, the mark or seal — and the World is the last trump, the seal of completion. The letter and the card say the same thing twice.

How to read a trump in full. A Major = its letter's image + its astrological force + its position on the Tree (which two sephiroth it bridges). The Empress = Daleth (door) + Venus (love, fertility) + the path joining Wisdom and Understanding = the generative doorway between the two supernal parents. Every trump resolves like this on the correspondence sheet.

Practice before the next lesson

Homework

  1. Sort the 22 by class. Lay out all the trumps and split them into three piles — element-trumps (3), planet-trumps (7), sign-trumps (12) — using the correspondence sheet to check.
  2. Letter → picture. For three trumps, look up the letter's pictographic meaning (ox, house, door, water, cross…) and find how it echoes in Smith's image.
  3. Trace two paths. Pick two trumps and, on your Tree drawing from Lesson 5, mark the paths they occupy — notice which states each one bridges.
  4. Read the primary source: the Sefer Yetzirah (short, and free online) on the three mothers, seven doubles, and twelve simples; then Robert Wang or Lon Milo DuQuette on the trumps' attributions.

Why sort into three piles: grouping 22 items into 3 + 7 + 12 chunks them into memory-sized units — far easier to hold than a flat list of twenty-two.

The Tree of Life: The Deck's Blueprint

By the end of this lesson You'll see the single diagram that has been quietly organizing everything: the Qabalistic Tree of Life. Its ten spheres are your ten numbers, its four Worlds are your four suits, and its twenty-two paths are your twenty-two trumps. The whole 78-card deck is one map.

The promise being paid off

Twice now I've pointed forward — the pips are numbered by their "sephirah" (Lesson 3), the trumps are "doors on the Tree of Life" (Lesson 2). This is where those promises come due. The Tree of Life is the master diagram of Hermetic Qabalah, and the Golden Dawn built the entire tarot onto it. Learn the Tree and the deck's structure stops being a list to memorize and becomes a place you can navigate.1

The map

Ten spheres (sephiroth), numbered 1–10 from the crown down to the material world, joined by twenty-two paths, arranged in three vertical pillars:

Three mappings — the whole deck, placed

1. The ten sephiroth = the ten numbers

Each numbered pip is its sephirah. That's why, in Lesson 3, five meant conflict (Geburah, Severity) and six meant harmony (Tiphareth, Beauty, the balanced centre). An Ace sits at Kether — the crown, pure potential — so it's the undivided root of its element. A Ten sits at Malkuth — the kingdom — so it's the element fully manifest in the material world. The number line you learned is a descent down the Tree, from crown to ground.

2. The four Worlds = the four suits

The Tree exists in four "Worlds" — descending levels of reality. Each is one of your suits/elements. So the forty pips are the ten sephiroth expressed four times over, once in each World:

WorldMeaningSuitElement
AtziluthEmanation — pure archetypeWandsFire
BriahCreation — the mind of the divineCupsWater
YetzirahFormation — the astral, forces taking shapeSwordsAir
AssiahAction — the physical, manifest worldPentaclesEarth

Ten sephiroth × four Worlds = 40 pips. The system is that tight.

3. The twenty-two paths = the twenty-two trumps

The paths are the lines connecting the spheres — the channels of movement between states. Each of the 22 Major Arcana is one path, so a trump is always about transition: the energy that carries you from one sephirah to another. The Fool (path 11) joins Kether to Chokmah — the very first step out of unity. Each trump also takes one Hebrew letter and one astrological attribution, all laid out on the master correspondence sheet.

The sixteen court cards live on the Tree too — the Golden Dawn seats the Kings/Knights at Chokmah, Queens at Binah, Princes at Tiphareth, Princesses at Malkuth. We'll return to that; for now, hold the big three mappings.

Why this is the master key. Numbers, suits, trumps, Hebrew letters, planets, zodiac signs, elements — every correspondence in tarot is ultimately a coordinate on this one diagram. Once a card has a place on the Tree, all its other attributions follow. This is the spine your whole mission is built around.

Retrieval practice

From memory: state the three mappings between the Tree and the deck.

  1. 10 sephiroth = the 10 numbers (Ace = Kether … Ten = Malkuth).
  2. 4 Worlds = the 4 suits (10 × 4 = 40 pips).
  3. 22 paths = the 22 Major Arcana (each trump a transition).

Which World, and which element, goes with each suit?

Wands = Atziluth / Fire · Cups = Briah / Water · Swords = Yetzirah / Air · Pentacles = Assiah / Earth.

Practice before the next lesson

Homework

  1. Draw the Tree from memory. Ten circles, three pillars, numbered 1–10 from Kether at the crown to Malkuth at the base. Then check it against the diagram above. Redraw once more without looking.
  2. Descend a suit. Lay one suit's Ace → Ten onto your drawn Tree, Ace on Kether down to Ten on Malkuth. Read the story of that element descending from pure potential into full material form.
  3. Trace a trump as a path. Pick any Major, look up the two sephiroth it connects on the correspondence sheet, and draw that line on your Tree. Notice which two states it bridges.
  4. Read the primary source: the opening chapters of Dion Fortune's The Mystical Qabalah (on the sephiroth), or Robert Wang's The Qabalistic Tarot if you have it — the definitive tarot-on-the-Tree text.

Why draw it by hand, twice: constructing the diagram yourself (not just viewing it) is what moves it from recognition into durable, usable memory.

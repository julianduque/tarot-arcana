Your First Reading: Three Cards, Read Honestly

By the end of this lesson You'll lay and read a real three-card spread — reading each card from the structure you've learned, weaving the three into one coherent story, and doing it ethically: honestly, and in a way that leaves the querent their agency. This is the whole first arc, put to use.

From studying to reading

You've surveyed all 78 cards and seen the Tree they hang on. Now you use them. A reading is just three moves: frame a question, draw cards into positions, and read each card in its position, then weave. Every skill from Lessons 1–5 feeds the third move.

1. Frame a good question

The question shapes everything. Good questions are open — they explore how and why and what if. Weak questions are closed (yes/no) or try to fix the future to a date, both of which deny the querent's agency and back the reader into fortune-telling.

  • ✓ "What's really going on with my career change, and how should I approach it?"
  • ✗ "Will I get the job?" · ✗ "What day will I be promoted?"

2. Draw into a three-card spread

Shuffle while holding the question, then lay three cards left to right. A clear, agency-preserving layout is Situation → Action → Outcome:

  • Situation — the heart of what's going on now.
  • Action — the approach or attitude to take.
  • Outcome — where that approach tends to lead (a tendency, not a fixed verdict).
1Situation
2Action
3Outcome

Lay them left to right; card 1 is the present, card 3 the tendency it's heading toward.

3. Read each card — then weave. A worked example

Question: "I'm struggling financially and feeling shut out — how do I move through this?" The draw:

Read each from structure first:

  • Situation — 5 of Pentacles: Earth (material life) × 5 (Geburah: conflict, loss). Material hardship, feeling excluded — and note the lit window they're walking past: help is nearer than they think.
  • Action — The Star: a Major, so a big force is the right response, not a tactic. Hope, healing, faith after hardship; open up, replenish, trust.
  • Outcome — 10 of Pentacles: Earth × 10 (Malkuth: full manifestation). Lasting material security, stability, family and legacy — the hardship of the Five resolved into abundance.

Now weave: "You're in a genuinely cold patch materially, and it's easy to feel shut out (5 of Pentacles). The way through isn't a clever financial move — it's the Star: lift your eyes, restore hope, and let people help (that lit window). Do that, and the tendency is toward real, lasting security — the Ten of Pentacles."

A first taste of elemental dignities. Look at the elements side by side: Earth, Air, Earth. The two Pentacles cards (both Earth) reinforce each other — the reading is strongly about the material world. The Star (Air) sitting between them lifts the material problem onto a different plane (hope, mind, spirit). Neighbouring cards strengthen, soften, or tension each other by element — same element reinforces, opposites (Fire↔Water, Air↔Earth) clash. That technique gets its own lesson later.

A second reading — when the elements clash

The first draw's elements reinforced each other. Watch what happens when they fight. Question: "Should I pursue this new relationship, and how?"

Read, then weave: A tender connection is forming (2 of Cups, Water) and it genuinely could grow into deep emotional fulfilment (10 of Cups, Water). But look at the Action: the Knight of Wands is Fire, sitting between two Water cards — contrary on both sides, so it reads ill-dignified. The message: a headlong, impatient, all-guns pursuit (the fiery Knight) is exactly wrong for what this gentle, watery bond needs — the Fire gets doused. "Yes, pursue it — but not like the Knight of Wands. Slow down and meet the water on its own terms." Notice the dignities didn't just colour the card; they reversed the advice the card would give upright and alone.

Read ethically — the part that matters most

This is central to your mission. A reading can help or harm depending on how it's held:

  • Preserve agency. Read tendencies and choices, never fixed fate. "This is where it's heading if nothing changes" — not "this will happen."
  • Empower, don't frighten. Every "hard" card (Tower, Death, 10 of Swords) has a constructive reading — change, ending, release. Fear helps no one.
  • Stay in your lane. Don't diagnose illness, predict death, or give legal/financial/medical verdicts. Refer people to qualified professionals.
  • Consent & privacy. Read for someone only if they want it; don't read to snoop on absent third parties.
  • Reading for yourself: watch the pull to see what you want. Name the structure of each card before you interpret, to keep yourself honest.

Practice — your first real reading

Homework

  1. Do a reading. Frame one open question about your own life. Shuffle, draw three, and lay them Situation → Action → Outcome. Read each card from its structure first (arcanum, suit/element, number or rank), then weave the three into one honest paragraph.
  2. Journal it. Write the question, the three cards, your reading, and one line on what you'll actually do. Dated entries become a feedback loop you can revisit.
  3. Spot the dignities. Note the element of each of your three cards. Any same-element reinforcement? Any opposites in tension? See how that colours the story.
  4. Re-read the hardest card in your draw in an empowering, agency-preserving way — practise turning fear into direction.

Why journal: a reading you can revisit later tells you whether your interpretations actually held — the only real way to improve.

Test it in the world (wisdom)

Skill grows against other readers. When a reading puzzles you, or you want to pressure-test an interpretation, bring it to a community: r/tarot is large and active (good for "here's my spread, what am I missing?"), and Mary K. Greer's blog is among the highest-trust living sources on reflective, ethical reading. Post the cards and your reading first — you'll learn far more than by asking others to read for you.

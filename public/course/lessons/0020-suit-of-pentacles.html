The Suit of Pentacles: Earth, Ace to King

By the end of this lesson You'll read the whole suit of Pentacles as one story — Earth building from the Ace's seed to the Ten's legacy — and know its four court people. The suit of the body, work, and the material world, top to bottom. This completes every card in the deck.

Pentacles = Earth (Assiah): body, money, work, matter

Pentacles (Disks) are the suit of Earth — the World of Assiah, the physical. Their arena is the material: money, work, health, home, skill, the tangible results of effort. Slowest and most grounded of the suits, Pentacles are where things actually get built. When they fill a reading, the matter is practical and real-world.

Earth's arc is the most constructive in the deck: from the Ace's seed, through honest work (3, 8) and a cold patch (5), to the Ten's enduring wealth and legacy. Slow, but it builds something that lasts.

The four Pentacles courts

CourtElement withinPersonality
PageEarth of EarthThe diligent student; a real opportunity to learn or earn
KnightAir of EarthSteady, methodical, reliable — the slowest, most dependable knight
QueenWater of EarthNurturing and practical; resourceful, warm, capable at home and work
KingFire of EarthMaterial mastery — the provider, the builder of lasting prosperity
The personality of Pentacles. Slow, solid, real. At best: security, craftsmanship, generosity, health, patience. At worst: greed, stagnation, materialism, fear of loss. Pentacles ask what are you building, and is it built to last?

Homework

  1. Walk the suit. Lay all 14 Pentacles Ace→King and tell the story: the seed, honest work, a cold patch at the Five, and lasting wealth by the Ten.
  2. Lay out all four suits together. Put the four Fives side by side, then the four Tens, then the four Queens — feel how the same number/rank changes across elements.
  3. Meet the court. Name someone who fits each of the four Pentacles courts.
  4. Read the primary source: Waite on the Pentacles in the Pictorial Key.

Why compare across suits at the end: seeing the same number in all four elements at once locks in number × element — the engine behind all forty pips.

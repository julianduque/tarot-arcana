The Court Cards: Element Within Element

Lesson 4 of 22 · Court Cards · Reading ~5 min · Practice separately

Interpret courts through their images and roles, then use an explicitly labelled elemental comparison to distinguish RWS from Thoth.

Start with behaviour rather than a personality label

A court may refer to a person, a way you are behaving, an available approach or a relationship between roles. “The Queen is a woman” prematurely narrows all those possibilities. Begin instead with what the figure does: holds, rides, attends, gestures, studies, rules. Clothing, age and gender are parts of the historical image, not requirements that a modern querent must meet.

Look at the RWS Pentacles courts. The Page studies the object; the Knight presents it from a comparatively still horse; the Queen inclines toward it; the King sits within a setting of established material authority. You might read these as learning, sustained work, attentive stewardship and governance. Those are interpretive possibilities anchored in differences you can point to. A suit label such as “money” would not explain them.

Declare the comparison before using it

This course uses Page = Earth, Knight = Air, Queen = Water and King = Fire when discussing RWS courts through the fourfold Hermetic model. That is the declared teaching crosswalk. It should not be attributed to an explicit complete table in Waite’s Pictorial Key, nor treated as a universal convention shared by every reader.

In Thoth the ranks are Princess = Earth, Prince = Air, Queen = Water and Knight = Fire. Therefore the functional comparison here pairs RWS Page with Thoth Princess, RWS Knight with Thoth Prince, RWS Queen with Thoth Queen and RWS King with Thoth Knight. The names alone are unsafe: “Knight of Cups” points to different places in these two rank systems. Name the deck every time a Knight is discussed across decks.

Element within element

Read “Water of Fire” as a rank quality working within a suit field. Fire is the arena of Wands; the Queen’s watery role concerns reception and holding. This can help you ask how warmth is sustained, how confidence affects others, and how attention can direct energy without imitating the King’s approach. The elements are a framework for comparing differences, not literal substances that mechanically cause a personality.

A doubled element deserves the same care. Queen of Cups, Water of Water, can amplify receptivity, imagination or immersion. It does not certify compassion in a real person. A receptive posture can become over-absorption; a decisive posture can become domination. Read the capacity and its limits in relation to the question rather than assigning a fixed good or bad type.

The rank element and suit element also serve different purposes in our beginner dignity method. A Knight of Wands is Air of Fire in the court crosswalk, but the simple three-card dignity exercise uses its suit, Fire. Later methods may weigh both levels. Do not quietly switch between them because a different result suits an interpretation.

Person, aspect or approach: an actual decision

Suppose RWS King of Swords appears in an advice position about a group disagreement. As an approach, it might invite explicit criteria, a clear decision and responsibility for its consequences. As an aspect of yourself, it might ask whether you are taking too much authority or avoiding it. As a person, it might bring a relevant decision-maker to mind. None of those interpretations follows from the rank alone.

Ask which possibility fits the agreed question and the facts already known. “Does this suggest a style of handling the disagreement?” is more open than “Which controlling man is causing the problem?” The second question invents both a character and a fault. If the querent recognizes no person, do not keep searching until someone can be forced into the role.

Compare without flattening the decks

Put the RWS Knight of Wands beside Thoth Prince of Wands, its counterpart in this course’s crosswalk. Record movement, setting and orientation before consulting the guide. Then compare each with Thoth Knight of Wands. A matching function is a reason to investigate, not permission to give the three images the same reading. The suit chapters provide full studies of each RWS court and a labelled Thoth comparison.

For Deviant Moon, repeat the procedure with Valenza’s own courts. Do not assign his figures a creator-intended elemental mechanism merely because it is useful to you as a reader. You can say “I am applying this course’s framework to the image” and still make a serious comparison.

Check your reasoning

These recognition checks support practice. They do not certify interpretive skill.

Which is the Air court in the Thoth system?

Thoth Prince is Air; Knight is Fire; Princess is Earth.

What should you establish before treating a court as a person?

Context is needed; a card does not establish facts about an absent person.

RWS King = Fire is presented here as what?

The convention is useful when labelled and used consistently.

Retrieve before revealing

Why is “Thoth Knight = RWS King” incomplete?

It describes the functional crosswalk used here. The figures have their own visual details and reading traditions; the statement does not establish complete equivalence.

Which element does our introductory dignity method use for RWS Knight of Wands?

Fire, the suit element. Its Air-of-Fire court attribution remains useful for character study, but does not replace the suit in that simplified method.

Practice and spaced review

  1. Lay four courts from one suit. Describe a different behaviour for each before naming an element.
  2. Compare RWS Knight and King with Thoth Prince and Knight of the same suit. Record deck names explicitly.
  3. Use one court in an advice position three ways: person, aspect, approach. Identify which needs more information from the querent.

At your next session, recall one distinction from this lesson before opening it. Revisit it about a week later and again when a subsequent card study uses it. Mark uncertain material for review rather than treating a lookup as failure.

Read with the sources

Primary text: Waite, Part III: King of Wands (continue through the suit’s courts). Compare the figure’s description with the interpretive list. Do not treat appearance-based historical categories as modern requirements.

Primary historical publication: The Equinox I:8 (1912), Liber LXXVIII: A Description of the Cards of the Tarot. Consult the card’s named section and attribution table. This publication transmits Book T material with editorial interventions; it is not an untouched original manuscript.

Primary text, archive transcription: Crowley, The Book of Thoth. Consult the named card entry in your edition; card studies provide additional section links. Compare the text with Harris’s painting, and distinguish later diagram conventions.

Creator and publisher: Patrick Valenza, Deviant Moon Tarot companion. Use your deck’s booklet or the matching companion entry. The publisher describes the book; it does not provide every card’s full explanation. Where you lack the text, mark your own visual interpretation as such.

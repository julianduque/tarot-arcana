Majors Act I: The Conscious World (1–7)

Lesson 13 of 22 · Major Arcana · Reading ~25 min · Practice separately

Distinguish the first seven trumps through close image study, Golden Dawn relationships and explicit Thoth comparisons. Study two or three cards per sitting.

This chapter includes full card studies. Read the core first, then study a few cards per sitting. RWS artwork, Golden Dawn correspondences and Thoth comparisons are explicitly labelled.

Read an image before its correspondence stack

These seven cards concern capacities and relationships through which a person acts in a shared world: skill, receptivity, generation, authority, teaching, union and direction. The three-act arrangement gives a memory scaffold; the individual studies prevent it from reducing each trump to a stage in a compulsory life story.

For each study, first examine Smith’s image. Then read the RWS interpretation, the labelled Golden Dawn layer and the Thoth comparison. Record one point of agreement and one difference. A source statement and a contemporary application are identified separately so that a useful modern reading is not accidentally attributed to Waite or Crowley.

Explore the correspondences

Golden Dawn framework. RWS and Thoth names are shown separately. The diagram’s convention is always labelled.

Compare relationships, not just isolated keywords

Priestess and Hierophant both concern knowledge, but their settings, gestures and relationships to others differ. An inward or veiled relation to knowledge is not the same as receiving a formal teaching. Consider when each mode is useful and when each can become limiting: privacy can protect reflection or prevent examination; tradition can sustain a practice or enforce an inherited rule without thought.

Empress and Emperor invite comparison between generation and ordering. Avoid reducing that relation to how women and men supposedly behave. The historical images use gendered symbols; a reading can explore the capacities those symbols represent in any person or organization. Ask what is being sustained, what is being bounded and how authority is exercised.

Lovers and Chariot both raise questions about a course of action, but commitment through relation differs from holding a direction. A decision can be internally coherent and still require discipline to enact. Conversely, strong forward movement can conceal a poorly examined choice. These comparisons help you read combinations without making every trump mean “use your will.”

Thoth deserves its own reading

The Thoth panels retain Crowley and Harris’s names and emphases. Magus, Priestess and the other titles are printed alongside the RWS names. The Lovers’ alchemical imagery will be returned to in Temperance/Art in lesson 14. Emperor’s Tzaddi attribution is shown without silently changing the Golden Dawn diagram’s endpoints.

12: Kether ↔ Binah

Observe the RWS image

A standing figure occupies the center of a garden, wearing a white tunic under a red robe. The raised right hand holds a pale wand, while the left points down toward the ground. A sideways figure-eight appears above the head, and the belt takes the form of a serpent meeting its own tail. A table carries a cup, sword, pentacle and wand, making the four suit emblems available in one place. Red roses frame the upper border and mix with pale lilies below. The posture is active but controlled. Notice that the tools remain on the table: the image establishes access and intention rather than showing a finished product.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II connects the gestures with spiritual power received and communicated; Part III also includes practical skill. A contemporary application can therefore examine how intention becomes an intelligible act. Naming resources, practicing a technique, explaining a proposal or making a prototype are all plausible readings. The difficult range includes persuasive display without substance, controlling the narrative, or mistaking the possession of tools for competence. The card is especially useful when a querent says they have no options: inventory what is actually available before accepting that conclusion. Conversely, if someone claims they can achieve anything, ask for a demonstration and the limits of their method. Agency becomes credible when it produces observable work.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Beth and Mercury belong to path 12, joining Kether and Binah in the Golden Dawn diagram. Beth's conventional house association can help you imagine an idea acquiring a usable container; Mercury supplies a language of communication, exchange and dexterity. The path can be contemplated as movement from undifferentiated possibility toward an intelligible form. That is a reading strategy, not a causal derivation of every possible meaning. Mercury's adaptability also permits ambiguity: a message can clarify or manipulate. Use the attribution to generate paired hypotheses about communication, then return to the question and the visible tools to choose between them.

Thoth — The Magus

Crowley's Magus is Mercury as communication and creative activity, with an ape introducing distortion into the message. Harris's mobile figure and circulating implements replace Smith's stable arrangement behind a table. This matters doctrinally: expression makes an idea available but also subjects it to ambiguity. As a contemporary inference, compare a clear intention with the imperfect language, interface or performance through which others receive it. A persuasive explanation may organize a team while quietly excluding inconvenient facts. RWS foregrounds deliberate use of available tools; Thoth makes the medium's instability harder to overlook. Read the Magus by testing the relationship between message, instrument and effect, rather than assuming that a confident performance proves either mastery or deceit.

Distinguish a similar card

The Magician and the Emperor both exercise agency, but they organize different things. The Magician asks how to perform an operation with available means; the Emperor asks who sets the structure within which operations occur. A skilled individual can improvise successfully inside a badly governed organization. A well governed organization can still lack the skill to deliver a particular result. Identify which problem the spread is examining.

Try a focused reading exercise

List the four suit resources available for a current project: initiative, relationships, information and material support. Choose one visible action that uses at least two, and state how you will know it worked.

13: Kether ↔ Tiphareth

Observe the RWS image

A seated woman faces forward between a black pillar marked B and a white pillar marked J. Behind her hangs a patterned veil of palms and pomegranates. A crescent rests near her feet, while a horned crown enclosing a round disk repeats the lunar motif above. A cross appears on her chest. She holds a scroll marked TORA, partly hidden by the blue mantle flowing across her lap. Unlike the Magician, she does not point or demonstrate a tool. The image repeatedly alternates disclosure and concealment: the lettering can be read, but not the whole scroll; the pillars frame an entrance, but the veil interrupts the view beyond it.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II treats the Priestess through a specifically religious and mystical symbolism; Part III stresses unrevealed matters. Modern readings often translate this into intuition, private knowledge or receptive attention. That translation is useful when identified as interpretation. In practice, the card can ask for time to listen, permission to retain privacy, or recognition that the available account is incomplete. It can also expose passivity disguised as wisdom or confidence in a feeling that has not been checked. Silence does not authenticate a belief. When reading for someone else, ask what remains uncertain or unspoken rather than announcing that you know their secret. The partly concealed scroll offers a discipline of incomplete knowledge.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Gimel, the Moon and path 13 connect Kether with Tiphareth in the Golden Dawn arrangement. The conventional camel association offers a mnemonic for carrying something across a difficult interval; the long central path supports that contemplative image. Lunar reflection suggests knowledge received indirectly rather than seized through force. These associations belong to Hermetic Qabalah and should not be presented as the unchanging meaning of a Hebrew letter across Jewish traditions. As a reading exercise, ask what must be held steadily while understanding develops. The attribution invites receptivity, but it does not make an intuitive impression more reliable than contrary evidence.

Thoth — The Priestess

Crowley identifies the Priestess with elevated lunar symbolism and treats light itself as a veil. Harris gives her the bow of Artemis, also understood as an instrument of enchantment; nascent forms and a camel appear below. These details develop a doctrine of manifestation, rather than simply illustrating a woman who knows secrets. As a comparative inference, Smith's partly covered scroll marks the boundary of an available statement, while Harris's luminous veil suggests the difficulty of distinguishing an appearance from what it reveals. A hunch may be valuable because it is provisional. Protect that provisional quality while investigating it. Neither depiction establishes privileged access to another person's private thoughts, and brightness of experience need not equal depth of understanding.

Distinguish a similar card

The High Priestess and the Moon are both associated with indirect light, but their situations differ. The Priestess is seated at a structured threshold with a text; the Moon presents an uncertain route through a landscape. One can suggest holding a question quietly; the other can suggest moving carefully while perception is confused. Neither should be flattened into the statement that intuition is always correct.

Try a focused reading exercise

Write one current hunch in three columns: what I observed, what I inferred, and what I still need to learn. Keep the three categories separate before drawing another card.

14: Chokmah ↔ Binah

Observe the RWS image

The Empress sits on cushions in a cultivated landscape rather than inside a stone hall. A crown of stars rests above her loose hair, and her pale dress is patterned with red pomegranates. She holds a short scepter topped by a globe. A heart-shaped shield bearing the sign of Venus leans near the seat. Ripening grain fills the foreground; trees and flowing water appear behind her. The figure and setting share rounded, abundant forms. Notice the mixture of natural growth and human provision: there are plants, but also clothing, cushions, a scepter and a designed seat. The scene offers a material environment that supports life rather than an abstract emblem of beauty alone.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II emphasizes fecundity and embodied life; Part III combines fruitfulness with less comfortable possibilities. A contemporary reading can explore nourishment, making, hospitality, pleasure and the conditions that help something grow. It need not concern pregnancy or a literal mother. A team may need enough time and resources to develop its work; a person may need to receive care rather than keep producing. The difficult range includes overprotection, indulgence without limits, or care that creates dependency. Ask who supplies the labor behind apparent abundance. The card becomes more useful when cultivation is understood as an ongoing relationship with needs, seasons and limits rather than a promise that prosperity simply arrives.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Daleth, Venus and path 14 join Chokmah with Binah in the Golden Dawn Tree. The conventional door association can help you remember a passage into form, while Venus provides a symbolic vocabulary of attraction, relationship and aesthetic value. The bridge between Wisdom and Understanding supports a meditation on making as the meeting of impulse and receptivity. These are chosen analogies within the Hermetic system, not proof that a particular person is naturally maternal or that women must occupy a receptive role. Read generation broadly: gardens, communities, meals, images and working environments all require forms of sustained nourishment.

Thoth — The Empress

Harris places a pelican feeding its young and an alchemical white eagle near the Empress, whose robe carries bees. Crowley relates these to continuity of life and to the Empress's relation with the Emperor, including the white and red alchemical tinctures. Smith's grain field makes nourishment materially immediate; Harris's heraldic details embed it in a wider symbolic genealogy. The following application is an inference: care both sustains present life and makes future life possible, but the costs of that continuity need to be visible. Ask how a family, community or project replenishes those who nourish it. The pelican is a historical emblem, not a demand that good care must consume the caregiver.

Distinguish a similar card

The Empress and the Queen of Pentacles can both suggest practical care, yet they work at different scales in a reading. The Empress can frame the whole condition of generativity; the Queen often helps describe a particular way of managing material life. Ask whether the spread concerns the environment that permits growth or the habits of the person tending it. A Major does not automatically outweigh the specific evidence of a court card.

Try a focused reading exercise

Choose something you want to grow. Identify its actual need for time, space, food or materials, attention and rest. Name one form of care that would help and one that would become intrusive.

Observe the RWS image

A crowned, bearded ruler sits squarely on a stone throne decorated with ram heads. Red robes cover much of the body, but armor remains visible at the legs. One hand holds a scepter shaped like an ankh; the other carries a globe. Bare mountains rise behind the throne, and the setting is much less lush than the Empress's landscape. The front-facing posture, heavy seat and repeated angular shapes create a strong sense of fixed position. Look at the coexistence of ceremonial clothing and military protection. The figure is not merely comfortable: authority is represented as something defended and materially organized, with the mountain landscape reinforcing the impression of resistant boundaries.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II describes worldly executive power and intellectual rulership; Part III includes protection and stability. Contemporary applications can therefore address structure, responsibility, boundaries and the use of authority. In a project, the card may ask for a decision maker, a clear scope or a dependable agreement. In a personal reading, it may concern the capacity to protect time and enforce a limit. The difficult range includes domination, rigidity and protecting an institution after its purpose has been lost. Authority is not automatically competence. Ask what the structure protects, who can challenge it, and how its effects are assessed. This keeps the Emperor useful without reducing it to a literal father or a command to take control.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

In the Golden Dawn diagram, the Emperor is Heh, Aries, path 15, Chokmah–Tiphareth. In Thoth's card crosswalk, the Emperor is Tzaddi and remains Aries. Keep those two statements separately labeled. Crowley's own Emperor discussion still names Chokmah and Tiphareth, so a simple exchange of Tree endpoints would conceal a textual complication. Heh's window and Tzaddi's fishhook are conventional mnemonic glosses, not mechanisms that prove a reading. Aries can suggest initiating force, and the question becomes how initiative acquires an accountable form. If using a later Thelemic path diagram, name its author and convention rather than presenting it as the universal correction.

Thoth — The Emperor

Harris's Emperor holds ram-headed regalia and displays a red eagle, paired with the Empress's white eagle. Crowley reads the triangle formed by his upper body above the crossed legs as alchemical sulfur, emphasizing concentrated fiery initiative. This gives the comparison more specificity than a generic association with structure. As a practical inference, decisive force may establish an order but become destructive when prolonged beyond its purpose. Compare founding a rule with maintaining it, or declaring an emergency with knowing when it has ended. Crowley assigns Tzaddi and Aries yet still describes authority passing from Chokmah to Tiphareth. Preserve that textual complication rather than pretending the new letter settles the endpoints of every Thelemic Tree diagram.

Distinguish a similar card

The Emperor and the Hierophant both sit above others symbolically, but their authority operates differently. The Emperor establishes and enforces an order; the Hierophant transmits an accepted teaching or practice. A workplace can have clear management and confused values, or shared values and weak management. Ask whether the immediate issue is who decides, what tradition legitimizes the decision, or whether those two sources of authority disagree.

Try a focused reading exercise

Write one rule you currently enforce. State its purpose, who benefits, its cost, and the evidence that would make you revise it. Then compare the rule's founding moment with its present maintenance.

Observe the RWS image

A religious figure wearing a triple crown sits between two gray pillars. One hand holds a staff ending in a triple cross; the other is raised in blessing. Two kneeling figures face the teacher with their backs toward the viewer, and crossed keys appear near the foot of the seat. Their patterned garments introduce differences within an otherwise strongly ordered composition. The main figure is elevated, the students lower, and everyone occupies an established place. Unlike the High Priestess's concealed scroll, the action here is public and directed toward an audience. The scene makes transmission visible through posture, clothing, hierarchy and a shared ritual setting, even before any particular doctrine is named.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II distinguishes institutional religion from the Priestess's withdrawn mystery; Part III includes alliance and constraint. A modern reading may concern education, initiation into a community, professional standards, ceremonies or inherited values. A trustworthy teacher can spare a beginner unnecessary errors, while a rigid institution can treat obedience as understanding. Ask what is being transmitted and how the learner can test or question it. The card may invite apprenticeship, or it may expose a mismatch between professed values and lived practice. It does not automatically condemn tradition. In a relationship reading, explore the role of shared expectations and public commitments rather than predicting marriage from the image alone.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Vav, Taurus and path 16 link Chokmah to Chesed in the Golden Dawn arrangement. The conventional nail or hook association can serve as a memory aid for joining and fixing; Taurus suggests endurance and embodied continuity. The path offers a contemplative question about how insight becomes an organized tradition that can outlast one individual. That is a symbolic model, not evidence that a school's longevity proves its truth. A durable practice can preserve skill or preserve an error. Use the correspondence to ask what holds a community together, what the teaching makes possible, and whether its fixed forms still serve the work.

Thoth — The Hierophant

Harris places a dancing child within a pentagram inside a hexagram, with a sword-bearing woman before the Hierophant. Crowley connects these figures to the union of microcosm and macrocosm and his proclaimed Aeon of Horus. This transforms Smith's public scene of religious instruction into a specific Thelemic statement about initiation and renewal. The practical comparison is an inference: an inherited form can carry a principle that transforms it from within. A school survives by transmitting a vocabulary, but teaching succeeds when students can use it independently. Ask whether a tradition develops capable practitioners or only obedient repeaters. Neither an institution's age nor its declaration of a new era establishes the truth of its teaching.

Distinguish a similar card

The Hierophant teaches within a public structure; the Hermit holds a lamp from a solitary height. Both can offer guidance, but one emphasizes transmission through a shared form and the other the authority of developed insight. When choosing a teacher, consider which you need: a curriculum and community, a particular person's judgment, or both. Solitude alone does not certify insight, just as credentials alone do not guarantee good teaching.

Try a focused reading exercise

Identify a rule you learned from a tradition, school or profession. Explain the problem it solves, a case where it helps, and a case where following it mechanically would fail.

17: Binah ↔ Tiphareth

Observe the RWS image

Two unclothed human figures stand beneath a large winged figure with open arms. The man looks toward the woman; the woman turns her face upward. Behind the woman, a serpent coils around a leafy fruit tree. Behind the man stands a tree with flame-like forms. A mountain rises between them, and a large sun illuminates the scene above the angel. The pair do not touch, despite sharing the foreground. Their openness and the different directions of their attention create the main relationship in the image. Notice that there is no third human suitor being chosen or rejected: whatever meanings of choice you bring, Smith's picture presents a different arrangement from that familiar older motif.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II centers human love and deliberately rejects an older vice-versus-virtue explanation; Part III includes attraction and love. A contemporary values-and-choice reading is therefore an extension, not an exact summary of his account. The card can explore mutual recognition, willingness to be known, alignment between desire and commitment, or the difference between attraction and a workable relationship. Its difficult range includes incompatible values, idealization and choices made to avoid being alone. It does not establish a destined partner. In nonromantic questions, ask what must cooperate without erasing its differences. A partnership becomes meaningful when the participants can name both the attraction and the obligations they are choosing.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Zayin, Gemini and path 17 join Binah to Tiphareth in the Golden Dawn Tree. The conventional sword gloss is useful because a sword distinguishes as well as separates; Gemini provides a symbolic language for pairing, exchange and duality. A reading can therefore explore the distinctions needed before a meaningful union is possible. This does not mean that the card's meaning is mechanically generated by a letter. The image, the question and the chosen deck still matter. Treat the two figures as symbolic positions available to any person, not as a universal claim that all relationships must reproduce binary gender roles.

Thoth — The Lovers

Crowley pairs the Lovers with Art as stages of an alchemical operation. Harris's royal marriage, multiple paired figures and egg make the comparison with Smith's Edenic pair substantial. The Lovers differentiates the constituents and stages their encounter; Art develops their transformation into a combined result. As a practical inference, a collaboration should first establish what each participant contributes and where the differences are real. Prematurely declaring complete harmony can conceal the very material that makes the collaboration valuable. This is more precise than translating the card as romance alone. Keep the alchemical royal figures in their historical symbolic context while allowing a contemporary reading to address partnerships of any gender or a conflict of values within one person.

Distinguish a similar card

The Lovers and the Two of Cups can both concern relationship. The Two of Cups usually offers a more immediate scene of exchange between two people; the Lovers can frame the principles, values or larger commitment that make the exchange significant. Neither is a guarantee of compatibility. Ask whether the reading concerns the quality of a particular encounter or the choice of what kind of relationship to build.

Try a focused reading exercise

For a collaboration, list two real differences that should remain visible and one shared purpose. Explain what must be clarified at the Lovers stage before an Art-like synthesis would be possible.

Observe the RWS image

An armored figure stands within a stone-colored chariot under a canopy patterned with stars. A crown and star appear above the face, and crescent-shaped ornaments sit on the shoulders. The charioteer holds a wand-like object, despite Waite's prose referring to a sword. A black sphinx and a white sphinx rest at the front, facing outward in slightly different directions. There are no visible reins joining them to the driver's hands. A city stands behind the vehicle, while a winged emblem and another red device decorate its front. The composition suggests readiness and command more clearly than actual speed: the sphinxes are seated and the vehicle is shown head-on.

RWS interpretation and application

Waite's Part II treats the Chariot as conquest on manifest and intellectual planes, while explicitly limiting its spiritual attainment. Part III includes triumph and conflict. A modern reading can address coordinated effort, persistence, direction and the ability to hold competing demands within one project. It can also reveal overcontrol, aggression or the belief that every difficulty yields to more force. A successful launch does not prove that a leader understands the people affected by it. Ask what victory means, what must cooperate, and what is being excluded from the definition of success. This makes the card more discerning than a simple assurance that willpower will overcome all obstacles.

Golden Dawn framework and interpretive use

Cheth, Cancer and path 18 connect Binah to Geburah in the Golden Dawn diagram. Cheth's conventional enclosure association and Cancer's protective shell supply a useful mnemonic for a vehicle that contains and carries. The path invites reflection on how a form concentrates force. This is symbolic reasoning, not evidence that a zodiac sign determines someone's driving personality. A useful reading question is whether the boundary protects a shared purpose or prevents feedback. The chariot needs enough enclosure to hold together and enough responsiveness to move through its environment. Protection and direction can support one another without becoming indistinguishable.

Thoth — The Chariot

Thoth's armored charioteer bears an amethyst Grail beneath a canopy, with four composite sphinxes representing combinations of the elements. Crowley describes the figure as enthroned within an already balanced vehicle rather than actively steering it. Smith's design makes command and outward triumph easier to read; Harris makes the protected cargo central. As a contemporary inference, ask what you carry that makes disciplined movement worthwhile. Impressive self-control can serve an aim that deserves revision, while valuable work may need stronger boundaries to survive distraction. The Grail belongs to Crowley's religious symbolism; this practical examination of purpose is an application of it. Keep the distinction between holding a sacred charge and winning a contest visible.

Distinguish a similar card

The Chariot and Strength both concern power, but the Chariot organizes movement toward an objective while Strength explores the quality of contact with a living force. A deadline may require Chariot-like coordination; a frightened colleague may require Strength-like patience. More pressure can improve the first situation and damage the second. Describe the resistance before deciding which kind of power would help.

Try a focused reading exercise

Define one goal, two competing demands and the boundary that could hold them together. Then state what you are carrying that makes the goal worth pursuing, beyond the satisfaction of winning.

Return to an actual question

Choose a question about learning, responsibility or commitment. The exercise should change more than the adjectives you attach to the cards: a teacher in an available-support position differs from a teacher whose authority needs scrutiny.

Retrieve before revealing

How can Empress/Emperor be read without imposing gender stereotypes?

Examine generation, provision, order and authority as symbolic capacities. Historical gendered imagery does not restrict those capacities to a querent’s gender.

What does the path layer add to the Empress?

In the Golden Dawn diagram it names a relation between Chokmah and Binah. That can enrich reflection on generation and form; it does not establish a literal pregnancy or a single compulsory meaning.

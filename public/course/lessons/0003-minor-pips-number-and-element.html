Reading the Pips: Number × Element

By the end of this lesson You'll derive the meaning of any of the 40 numbered Minor cards instead of memorizing them — by combining two things you're already learning: the card's number and its element. Forty cards collapse into two small axes.

The beginner's wall — and the way through it

Most people stall on the Minor Arcana. The 22 trumps have vivid names; the 16 courts are "people"; but the forty pips — Two through Ten in four suits — feel like a wall of interchangeable cups and coins. The trap is trying to memorize forty separate meanings.

You won't. In the Hermetic system every pip is just an intersection of two axes you already know:

  • The suit → the element (from Lesson 1): the arena of life — Fire/will, Water/emotion, Air/mind, Earth/matter.
  • The number → a keynote: what stage the situation is at. This is the card's place on the Tree of Life (its sephirah), distilled.

Meaning = number × element. Learn ten number-keynotes and four elements, and all forty pips become readable.

The number line (Ace to Ten)

Each number carries a keynote — the compressed essence of its sephirah on the Tree of Life. You don't need the Qabalah yet; you need the keynote:

#SephirahKeynote
Ace / 1KetherPure potential — the raw seed of the element
2ChokmahInitiation — first expression, union, a choice
3BinahEstablishment — the idea takes form, growth
4ChesedStability — structure, consolidation, rest
5GeburahConflict — disruption, loss, challenge
6TipharethHarmony — balance, success, the centre restored
7NetzachChallenge — effort against odds, imbalance
8HodMovement — adjustment, analysis, speed
9YesodNear-fruition — intensity, the "almost there"
10MalkuthFullness — complete manifestation, the cycle's end

A rough rhythm to feel: odd numbers (5, 7) tend to disturb; the calm-points (4 stability, 6 harmony) tend to settle; 10 is fullness that can tip into excess.

The move, demonstrated: the four Fives

Watch one number wear four elements. Five = Geburah = conflict/loss. Now apply that keynote to each arena — same disruption, four very different rooms:

You didn't memorize four cards. You took one keynote (5 = conflict) and moved it through four arenas. Smith even paints the difference: brawling bodies for Fire, spilled cups for Water, seized blades for Air, cold poverty for Earth.

Two more, worked out loud

3 of Swords = 3 (establishment, giving form) × Air (mind, thought). Give sorrow a fixed form in the mind and you get the pierced heart: grief made conscious, heartbreak named. Smith draws it almost literally.

10 of Pentacles = 10 (full manifestation) × Earth (matter, wealth). The material world fully realized: lasting wealth, family, legacy, security passed down. The card is crowded with permanence.

Every pip already has a "true name." The Golden Dawn gave each a title that encodes its decan — 3 of Swords is "Sorrow" (Saturn in Libra), 5 of Cups is "Disappointment" (Mars in Scorpio), 10 of Pentacles is "Wealth" (Mercury in Virgo). Those titles, and the astrology behind them, live on the correspondence sheet — the Minor Arcana track will earn them next. For now, number × element is enough to read every pip.

Three decks, three ways of showing a pip

Rider-Waite-Smith

Smith's 1909 innovation: every pip is a little scene you can read at a glance. Easiest to learn from.

Thoth

Geometric pips with the keyword and astrology printed on the card (e.g. "Disappointment"). Wears the number × element system openly.

Deviant Moon

Illustrated scenes in the RWS lineage, so your number × element reading carries straight over — with its own surreal spin.

Cups = Water, so a Five of Cups is conflict in the realm of feeling: loss. (Fire = 5 Wands, Earth = 5 Pentacles.)

Retrieval practice

From memory: give the one-word keynote for numbers 2, 4, 5, 6, and 10.

2 initiation · 4 stability · 5 conflict · 6 harmony · 10 fullness.

Derive it yourself: what does the Five of Swords mean, from number × element?

5 (conflict) × Swords (Air / the mind) = conflict of the intellect: defeat, hollow or dishonest victory, a fight that costs more than it wins.

Practice before the next lesson

Homework

  1. Derive before you check. Pull the four Twos from a deck. Before opening any book, say what each means from number × element (2 = initiation/choice, applied to Fire, Water, Air, Earth). Then check them against the pictures and a guide — note where your derivation was right.
  2. Walk one suit's number line. Lay one full suit Ace → Ten in order and read it as a single story of that element moving from seed (Ace) to fullness (Ten).
  3. Cross-deck the correspondence. Find 5 of Cups in RWS (a mourning scene) and in Thoth (geometry labelled "Disappointment"). See how Thoth prints the number × element meaning right on the card.
  4. Read the primary source: the pip entries in Waite's Pictorial Key (Part III, the Lesser Arcana) — compare his terse meanings to what you derived.

Why derive before checking: predicting the answer first (a "generation effect") builds far stronger memory than reading the answer cold.

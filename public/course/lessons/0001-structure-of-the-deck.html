The Fourfold Key: How the 78 Cards Are Built

By the end of this lesson You'll be able to pick up any card from any of your three decks and instantly place it in the deck's structure — Major or Minor, which suit, and which of the four elements it carries. That placement is the hook every correspondence in this course will hang on.

Why this is lesson one

You own three decks that look wildly different — Smith's storybook figures, Harris's Art-Deco Thoth, Valenza's moonlit grotesques. Under the paint they are the same machine. Before memorizing what a single card "means," you learn the frame that holds all 78, because in the Hermetic tradition a card's meaning is largely derived from its position in that frame — its suit, its number, its element. Learn the skeleton first and later cards almost teach themselves.1

Two arcana: 22 + 56 = 78

Every tarot deck splits in two. The Major Arcana is 22 numbered trumps (0–21), from The Fool to The World — the big, archetypal forces. The Minor Arcana is the other 56 cards, organized into four suits — the texture of ordinary life.

Hold the split in your head as a simple tree:

Tarot deck — 78 cards
├── Major Arcana — 22 trumps (0 The Fool … 21 The World)
└── Minor Arcana — 56 cards, in four suits of 14
    └── each suit: Ace–Ten (10 pips) + Page, Knight, Queen, King (4 courts)
        → 4 suits × 14 = 56

Four suits, four elements

The four suits are not decoration — each is one of the four classical elements. This is the single most useful correspondence in tarot, because it never changes across decks and it colours the whole suit. Read the pictures, not just the labels. Smith paints the element into every Ace: the Wand sprouts leaves (living fire/growth), the Cup overflows (feeling brimming over), the Sword cuts the air and is crowned (the sharp, deciding mind), the Pentacle sits above a fertile garden (wealth rooted in the earth). The suit tells you the arena of life a Minor card speaks to.

A dispute worth knowing now. This course uses the Golden Dawn attribution — Wands = Fire, Swords = Air — which your Rider-Waite-Smith and Thoth decks both follow. A minority tradition swaps them (Wands = Air, Swords = Fire). When you read older or eclectic books, check which convention they use so you're not quietly working from a different map.2

The "fourfold key" — a first glimpse

Why four suits, and why does four keep recurring — four elements, four court ranks (Page, Knight, Queen, King)? Because the whole Minor Arcana is built on the four letters of the Hebrew name of God, the Tetragrammaton יהוה (Yod-Heh-Vav-Heh): Fire, Water, Air, Earth. One fourfold pattern, repeated at the scale of the suits and inside each suit's court cards.

You don't need to work that machinery yet — that's the Correspondences track ahead. For now just register the shape: four is the key, and every suit is one element. That single fact is your win today.

Same skeleton, three decks

Your decks agree on the structure and disagree on the names. Knowing the translation now saves confusion later:

Rider-Waite-Smith

Suits: Wands, Cups, Swords, Pentacles. Courts: Page, Knight, Queen, King. Strength = VIII, Justice = XI.

Thoth

Suits: Wands, Cups, Swords, Disks. Courts: Princess, Prince, Queen, Knight. Some trumps renamed (e.g. Strength → Lust).

Deviant Moon

Follows the RWS structure and names (Pentacles; Page–Knight–Queen–King), with its own surreal imagery. The safest deck to cross-check against RWS.

So "Pentacles" and "Disks" are the same suit (Earth), and a Thoth Knight is an RWS King. Same machine, different faceplates — exactly the court trap the correspondence sheet lays out in full.

Check your understanding

How is a full tarot deck divided?

22 archetypal trumps plus 56 Minor cards (four suits of fourteen) makes 78.

Which suit carries the element of Water?

Cups = Water: emotion, love, and intuition — the overflowing chalice of the Ace.

The Page, Knight, Queen, and King are known as the —

The 16 court cards are the "people" of the Minor Arcana — four ranks per suit.

Your RWS "Pentacles" appear as "Disks" in which deck?

Thoth (and Marseille) call the Earth suit Disks or Coins; RWS and Deviant Moon say Pentacles.

Retrieval practice

Without scrolling up: name the four suits and the element each one carries.

  • Wands — Fire (will, energy, spirit)
  • Cups — Water (emotion, love, intuition)
  • Swords — Air (intellect, conflict, truth)
  • Pentacles / Disks — Earth (body, money, matter)

From memory: what are the two arcana, and how many cards in each?

The Major Arcana — 22 trumps (0–21). The Minor Arcana — 56 cards, four suits of 14 (ten pips + four courts each). 22 + 56 = 78.

Practice before the next lesson

Homework

  1. Sort your deck by hand. Take any one of your three physical decks and physically separate it into the two arcana, then split the Minor Arcana into its four suits. Feel the 22 / 56 split and the four families in your hands — this is the skeleton becoming muscle memory.
  2. Lay the four Aces in a row and name each one's element out loud, pointing to the clue in the picture (sprouting wand, overflowing cup, crowned sword, garden coin).
  3. Cross-deck check. Find the Earth suit in all three decks — note that Thoth calls it Disks while RWS and Deviant Moon say Pentacles. Same suit, different faceplate.
  4. Read the primary source: the opening sections of A. E. Waite, The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1911) on the division of the cards — the free, full text by the man who commissioned your RWS deck. You'll recognize the structure you just learned, in his own words.

Why by hand: physically sorting builds storage strength (long-term retention) far better than re-reading. Do it once and the frame sticks.

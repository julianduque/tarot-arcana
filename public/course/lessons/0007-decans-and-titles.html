The 36 Decans: Planets, Signs & Card Titles

Lesson 7 of 22 · Minor Arcana · Reading ~5 min · Practice separately

Locate a pip’s decan and ruler, distinguish Golden Dawn from Thoth titles, and explain why the attribution adds a layer rather than mechanically producing the meaning.

Learn the vocabulary before the table

The zodiac is conventionally divided into twelve signs of 30 degrees. Each sign has an element and a modality. Cardinal signs initiate a seasonal phase, fixed signs occupy its middle, and mutable signs lead toward the next. Here these terms identify a traditional classification, not a personality judgement about people born under a sign.

A decan is a ten-degree portion of a sign. Three decans per sign make thirty-six around the full circle. In the Golden Dawn tarot arrangement, the thirty-six pips from Two through Ten occupy those sectors. An Ace represents an elemental root and has no single decan. “Mars in Aries” on a tarot card is a symbolic attribution; it does not tell you where Mars is in the sky today or describe the querent’s birth chart.

Two patterns locate a card

Within each suit, Twos, Threes and Fours occupy the three decans of its cardinal sign; Fives, Sixes and Sevens its fixed sign; Eights, Nines and Tens its mutable sign. Wands: Aries, Leo, Sagittarius. Cups: Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces. Swords: Libra, Aquarius, Gemini. Pentacles/Disks: Capricorn, Taurus, Virgo. The zodiac wheel does not therefore run through an entire suit before changing to another.

A separate planetary cycle assigns rulers. Starting with Mars at the first Aries decan, continue Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, then repeat. This is a rotation of the Chaldean planetary order. The seven-step cycle does not divide evenly into thirty-six: do not infer that the last and first decans form a seamless seven-step loop. This sequence is also not the ordinary weekday order; planetary-hour schemes explain that separate relationship.

Explore the decans

Golden Dawn framework. RWS and Thoth names are shown separately. The diagram’s convention is always labelled.

Try Five of Pentacles. Five places it in the first decan of the fixed Earth sign, Taurus: 0–10 degrees Taurus. Continuing the rulers from Aries places Mercury there. You have located the attribution. You have not yet explained its historical title or what Smith’s cold scene can mean in a specific reading. Those require further comparison.

Planets, signs and different kinds of rulership

For initial study, treat the seven classical planets as functions: Sun, illumination and centrality; Moon, change and reception; Mercury, exchange and articulation; Venus, attraction and relation; Mars, assertion and division; Jupiter, expansion and ordering; Saturn, limit and duration. These introductory descriptions help you ask questions; they do not exhaust traditional astrology.

Distinguish a sign’s ruler from the planet ruling one decan within it. Distinguish both from a planet associated with a sephirah. A card can carry several planetary relationships at different levels. You will encounter apparent tensions because the layers are not one flat list. Essential dignity, such as a planet’s domicile or exaltation, is an astrological condition. Tarot elemental dignity is a relationship between neighbouring cards. Neither term should silently substitute for the other.

Separate historical and Thoth titles

The reference gives two named title columns. Golden Dawn Five of Cups is Loss in Pleasure; Thoth prints Disappointment. Golden Dawn Seven of Swords is Unstable Effort; Thoth prints Futility. Golden Dawn Seven of Pentacles is Success Unfulfilled; Thoth prints Failure. Some titles agree and some change. The comparison is part of the teaching, not a footnote to conceal.

A card’s title should be understood in its source. It is not its one “true name.” The nineteenth- and twentieth-century traditions we study selected emphases and sometimes revised them. Crowley’s published material also develops over time. For example, the 1912 Liber 78 material already contains commentary that complicates a wholly happy reading of Ten of Cups before the later Thoth deck’s Satiety.

Work through a tension rather than explaining it away

Five of Cups has Mars in Scorpio. An overly simple rule might expect a planet in its own sign to make the card automatically favourable. Yet the tarot tradition gives the card a difficult title. This is a useful warning: a whole card cannot be derived from a single astrological dignity. Number, World, inherited description and the author’s interpretation contribute additional constraints.

Likewise, Mercury in Sagittarius does not independently prove the exact narrative on RWS Eight of Wands. The correspondence offers language for movement, transmission and direction, while the image gives a particular arrangement to interpret. Describe where the layers agree and where they remain in tension. Esoteric study becomes more informative when a difficult case is examined rather than forced into a neat slogan.

Use the wheel as a recall tool

Choose a card, predict its sign and degree range, then use the selector or a sector to check. Say the planet separately. Follow three consecutive sectors across a sign boundary and name the suit change. Reading along the zodiac and reading along a suit are two different orders through the same assignments. Work in small groups until the pattern is familiar, then add mixed retrieval in later suit studies.

Check your reasoning

These recognition checks support practice. They do not certify interpretive skill.

Where does Eight of Cups fall?

Eight begins the mutable sign of Water: Pisces 0–10°, with Saturn as decan ruler.

Which pairing preserves the title distinction?

Do not label a later deck title as if all earlier sources used it.

Which statement describes the planet printed on a Thoth pip?

A fixed card attribution is not a measurement of the present sky.

Retrieve before revealing

What distinguishes sign rulership from decan rulership?

A sign covers 30 degrees and has a traditional ruler. Each ten-degree division also receives a ruler in the chosen decan scheme. The same word “ruler” can therefore refer to different levels.

Why should “planet in sign” on a card not be read as a current transit?

It is the card’s symbolic attribution in this system. A real transit requires dated astronomical or astrological information independent of the draw.

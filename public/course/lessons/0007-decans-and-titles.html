The 36 Decans: A Pip's True Name

By the end of this lesson You'll add the astrological layer to the pips: each of the 36 numbered Minors (2–10) rules a decan — a planet in a sign — which gives it its Golden Dawn title and explains why it means what it does. You'll be able to place any pip's decan from a simple, regular pattern.

Paying off "the true name"

In Lesson 3 you learned to read a pip as number × element, and I promised each one already has a "true name" — the Golden Dawn title (5 of Cups = Disappointment). That title isn't arbitrary: it's the distillation of a decan, and now that you know the Tree, you're ready for it.1

What a decan is

Astrologers split each of the 12 zodiac signs into three 10° slices — decans — giving 36 in total. The 36 numbered pips (2 through 10, in four suits) map onto them exactly, one card per decan. Each decan carries two things:

  • A sign — the element and flavour (its suit already told you the element).
  • A planetary sub-ruler — a planet "in" that sign, following the old Chaldean order. The planet-in-sign is what gives the card its specific character.

The pattern is regular (this is the gift)

You don't memorize 36 random assignments. Within each element's suit the signs fall in a fixed order by modality:

CardsModalityWands (Fire)Cups (Water)Swords (Air)Disks (Earth)
2 · 3 · 4CardinalAriesCancerLibraCapricorn
5 · 6 · 7FixedLeoScorpioAquariusTaurus
8 · 9 · 10MutableSagittariusPiscesGeminiVirgo

So 2–3–4 = the cardinal sign of the element, 5–6–7 = the fixed sign, 8–9–10 = the mutable sign. Know a card's suit and number and you can place its sign instantly. (The Aces stand above the decans — each rules a whole element, not one slice.)

The planet is derivable too — the Chaldean order

The sign you now derive from the pattern. The planet isn't random either: the 36 decans march through the ancient Chaldean order of the seven classical planets, one per decan, continuously around the whole zodiac. The order is:

♂ Mars → ☉ Sun → ♀ Venus → ☿ Mercury → ☽ Moon → ♄ Saturn → ♃ Jupiter → (repeat)

Start at Mars on the 2 of Wands (Aries I) and walk this seven-step cycle forward, decan by decan, and you land on every card's planet. Aries: Mars, Sun, Venus (2–4 Wands). Then straight on into Taurus: Mercury, Moon, Saturn (5–7 Disks). Then Gemini: Jupiter, Mars, Sun (8–10 Swords) — and so on, the seven planets looping five-and-a-bit times across the 36. (It's the same Chaldean sequence that orders the days of the week.) So both halves of "planet-in-sign" are derivable — nothing here is memorized-by-rote.

One number, four planets, four names

Watch the four Fives again — all fixed-sign decans, but each with a different planet, and so a different Golden Dawn title. The number 5 (Geburah, conflict) refracts through four planet-in-sign combinations:

Saturn (restriction) in Leo (fire) grinds into Strife; Mars (aggression) in Scorpio (deep water) curdles into Disappointment. The planet-in-sign is the reason behind the keyword.

When the picture is the decan

8 of Wands: Mercury (speed, messages) in Sagittarius (the far-flung archer) = Swiftness — and Smith paints eight wands flying like arrows. 10 of Swords: the Ten (Malkuth, the end of the line) of the mental suit, Sun in Gemini = Ruin — the bottoming-out of Air. Once you know the decan, the image reads itself.

Your Thoth deck already shows this. Crowley prints the Golden Dawn title and the astrological glyphs (planet + sign) right on each small card. What feels like esoteric trivia here is literally written on your Thoth pips — this lesson is the key to reading them. The full 36 are on the master correspondence sheet.

Retrieval practice

From memory: which modality (and how many degrees) do the numbers 2–4, 5–7, and 8–10 take?

2–4 = the cardinal sign, 5–7 = the fixed sign, 8–10 = the mutable sign of the suit's element. Each card = one 10° decan.

Place the decan: what sign rules the 8 of Cups? (suit + number → element + modality)

Cups = Water; 8 → mutable. The mutable water sign is Pisces. (Its decan is Saturn in Pisces — "Indolence.")

Practice before the next lesson

Homework

  1. Place five decans from the pattern. For the 3 of Wands, 6 of Cups, 9 of Swords, 4 of Pentacles, and 7 of Wands, name the sign from suit + number (element + modality). Then check the correspondence sheet.
  2. Read your Thoth pips. Pull five small cards from your Thoth deck and read the title + astrology printed on each — you can now decode what those glyphs mean.
  3. Title vs. picture. For three RWS pips, say the Golden Dawn title, then find where Smith painted that mood into the scene.
  4. Read the primary source: the decan sections of Book T (in Regardie's The Golden Dawn), or Austin Coppock's 36 Faces for the decans in depth.

Why derive the sign before checking: the modality pattern is the whole point — using it (not looking it up) is what makes all 36 decans yours.
